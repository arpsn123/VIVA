TOPIC: Tokens

DEFINITION

A token is the basic unit of text processed by a Large Language Model.

LLMs do not read words directly.

Instead, text is broken into smaller units called tokens.

Examples:

"Hello"

may become:

["Hello"]

---

Complex words may be split.

Example:

"unbelievable"

may become:

["un", "believ", "able"]

depending on the tokenizer.

---

WHY TOKENS MATTER

LLMs operate entirely on tokens.

Every prompt is converted into tokens before processing.

Every response is generated token by token.

---

TOKENIZATION

Tokenization is the process of converting raw text into tokens.

Workflow:

Text

↓

Tokenizer

↓

Tokens

↓

LLM

Different models may use different tokenization strategies.

---

CONTEXT WINDOW

The context window of an LLM is measured in tokens.

Example:

8K tokens

32K tokens

128K tokens

1M tokens

The larger the context window, the more information the model can process simultaneously.

---

INPUT TOKENS

Tokens provided by the user.

Example:

"What is RAG?"

These consume part of the context window.

---

OUTPUT TOKENS

Tokens generated by the model.

Example:

A generated answer.

These also consume context window capacity.

---

TOKENS IN RAG

Retrieved context is inserted into prompts.

This retrieved context consumes tokens.

Large retrieved documents increase token usage.

For this reason:

Chunking
Retrieval
Context Management

are important.

---

INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

Q: What is a token?

A: The basic unit of text processed by an LLM.

Q: Why do tokens matter?

A: Context windows, inference costs, and prompt length are measured in tokens.

Q: What is tokenization?

A: The process of converting raw text into tokens before LLM processing.
