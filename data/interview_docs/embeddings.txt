TOPIC: Embeddings

OVERVIEW

Embeddings are dense numerical vector representations of data such as text, images, audio, videos, or documents. They convert complex information into a list of numbers that captures semantic meaning.

The core idea behind embeddings is that similar items should have similar vector representations and therefore appear close together in vector space.

Embeddings are one of the most important concepts in modern AI systems, especially in Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), semantic search, recommendation systems, clustering, and vector databases.

---

WHY DO WE NEED EMBEDDINGS?

Computers cannot directly understand human language.

Example:

Sentence 1:
"I love dogs."

Sentence 2:
"I like puppies."

Humans understand that both sentences have similar meanings.

However, computers see them as different strings.

Embeddings solve this problem by converting text into vectors that capture semantic meaning.

After embedding:

Sentence 1:
[0.12, -0.45, 0.91, ...]

Sentence 2:
[0.10, -0.43, 0.88, ...]

The vectors become close to each other because their meanings are similar.

---

WHAT IS A VECTOR?

A vector is an ordered list of numbers.

Example:

[1.2, 3.4, 5.6]

In AI, vectors can contain hundreds or thousands of dimensions.

Examples:

* 384 dimensions
* 768 dimensions
* 1024 dimensions
* 1536 dimensions
* 3072 dimensions

Each dimension captures some learned feature of the data.

---

WHAT IS VECTOR SPACE?

Vector Space is a mathematical space where embeddings exist.

Each document, image, sentence, or word becomes a point in this space.

Similar concepts appear close together.

Example:

King -------- Queen

Dog -------- Puppy

Car -------- Vehicle

Unrelated concepts appear far apart.

Example:

Dog -------- Airplane

Pizza -------- Quantum Physics

---

SEMANTIC MEANING

Semantic meaning refers to the actual meaning or intent behind text.

Traditional keyword search:

Query:
"Automobile"

Document:
"Car"

Keyword search may fail because the words differ.

Embedding search succeeds because the meanings are similar.

This is called Semantic Search.

---

WORD EMBEDDINGS

Word embeddings represent individual words as vectors.

Before embeddings:

Words are treated as independent symbols.

Example:

Cat = 1
Dog = 2
Car = 3

No relationship exists between them.

After embeddings:

Cat and Dog become close together because both are animals.

Cat and Car remain far apart.

Popular Word Embedding Models:

1. Word2Vec
2. GloVe
3. FastText

---

WORD2VEC

Developed by Google in 2013.

Purpose:
Learn vector representations of words.

Main Idea:

Words appearing in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings.

Example:

"The cat is sleeping."

"The dog is sleeping."

The model learns that cat and dog are related.

Two Architectures:

1. CBOW
2. Skip-Gram

---

CBOW

CBOW stands for Continuous Bag of Words.

Task:

Predict a missing word using surrounding words.

Example:

"The ____ is sleeping."

Model predicts:
cat

Advantage:
Fast training.

---

SKIP-GRAM

Task:

Predict surrounding words from a target word.

Example:

Target:
cat

Predict:
the, is, sleeping

Advantage:
Works well with rare words.

---

GLOVE

GloVe stands for Global Vectors.

Developed by Stanford.

Combines:

* Global word statistics
* Local context information

Advantages:

* Captures semantic relationships
* Efficient training

---

FASTTEXT

Developed by Facebook.

Represents words using character subwords.

Example:

Playing

Subwords:
Play
Lay
Ing

Advantages:

* Handles rare words
* Handles misspellings
* Works well for multiple languages

SIMILARITY SEARCH

Similarity search finds vectors that are most similar to a query vector.

Example:

Query:
"How do neural networks work?"

Search system compares the query embedding with stored document embeddings.

Documents with highest similarity scores are returned.

This is the foundation of RAG.

---

COSINE SIMILARITY

Most common similarity metric.

Measures angle between vectors.

Range:

-1 to 1

Values:

1
Perfectly similar

0
No similarity

-1
Opposite direction

Higher cosine similarity indicates greater semantic similarity.

---

EUCLIDEAN DISTANCE

Measures straight-line distance between vectors.

Smaller distance means greater similarity.

Formula:

Distance between two vectors in vector space.

Applications:

* Clustering
* KNN
* Vector search

---

DOT PRODUCT

Another similarity metric.

Measures alignment and magnitude between vectors.

Often used in:

* Deep learning
* Vector databases
* Recommendation systems

EMBEDDINGS IN RAG

RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation.

Workflow:

Step 1:
Convert documents into embeddings.

Step 2:
Store embeddings in a vector database.

Step 3:
Convert user query into an embedding.

Step 4:
Perform similarity search.

Step 5:
Retrieve relevant chunks.

Step 6:
Send retrieved context to the LLM.

Step 7:
Generate final answer.

Without embeddings, semantic retrieval would not be possible.

---

CHUNKING AND EMBEDDINGS

Large documents are usually split into chunks.

Example:

100-page PDF

Split into:

* Chunk 1
* Chunk 2
* Chunk 3

Each chunk receives its own embedding.

Benefits:

* Better retrieval
* Faster search
* More accurate context

COMMON INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

Q: What is an embedding?

A: An embedding is a dense numerical vector representation of data that captures semantic meaning.

Q: Why are embeddings important?

A: They allow machines to understand semantic relationships between pieces of data.

Q: What is the difference between keyword search and semantic search?

A: Keyword search relies on exact word matches, while semantic search uses embeddings to find similar meanings.

Q: What is Word2Vec?

A: A neural network model that learns vector representations of words based on context.

Q: Difference between CBOW and Skip-Gram?

A: CBOW predicts a word from context, while Skip-Gram predicts context from a word.

Q: What is cosine similarity?

A: A metric that measures similarity between vectors using the angle between them.

Q: Why are embeddings used in RAG?

A: They enable retrieval of semantically relevant documents from a knowledge base.

Q: What is chunking?

A: Splitting large documents into smaller sections before generating embeddings.

Q: What is a vector database?

A: A database optimized for storing and searching vector embeddings.

Q: Can embeddings be used for images?

A: Yes. Modern embedding models can represent text, images, audio, and video.
